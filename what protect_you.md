
## So What Actually Protects You?

Legal regimes, not marketing. Two words you'll leave with:

::: fragment
**SecNumCloud** (France, ANSSI). A certification, not a badge. 
Among the requirements: the provider must be 
**majority EU-owned and controlled**, with no foreign holding 
above the legal ceiling, EU-based staff for sensitive operations, 
and data staying on EU soil. Rejection is common. US parents are 
structurally disqualified. That's the point.
:::

::: fragment
**BSI C5** (Germany, Bundesamt für Sicherheit in der 
Informationstechnik). An audited cloud controls catalogue: 
encryption, personnel screening, where administration happens. 
Type 2 means the auditor checked the provider actually 
*did* the thing over time, not just wrote a policy about it.
:::

::: fragment
The insight to remember: **certification binds the company's 
ownership, not just its promises**. A German region on an 
American platform passes neither test. That asymmetry 
is the whole game.
:::

::: notes
Spoken version: "SecNumCloud doesn't ask 'is your datacenter in
France?' It asks 'who owns you, who administers the machines,
and under whose law can they be compelled?' C5 asks roughly the
same in German, with auditors."
If pressed on Brexit-era comparisons, stay humble: these are two
national schemes, not EU-wide law. That fragmentation is itself
worth a passing remark.
:::
