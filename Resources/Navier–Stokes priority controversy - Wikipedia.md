# Navier–Stokes priority controversy - Wikipedia

> ## Excerpt
> On 8 September 2026, artificial intelligence company OpenAI claimed a solution to the Navier–Stokes existence and smoothness problem.[1][2] However, approximately 12 hours before OpenAI's announcement, mathematician Tristan Buckmaster made a statement on behalf of himself and partly Levent Alpöge, claiming that their recent advances on Euler equations (a special case of Navier Stokes), reported in the same post,[3] were leaked to OpenAI a few days earlier and may have inspired OpenAI's prompts for the agents in the process that led to the solution.[4][5] OpenAI denied such direct use of their work, claiming "significant" differences in proof methods in the Euler case.[6][7]

### Work before Buckmaster and Alpöge

[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Navier%E2%80%93Stokes_priority_controversy&action=edit&section=2 "Edit section: Work before Buckmaster and Alpöge")

The [Navier–Stokes equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Navier%E2%80%93Stokes_equations "Navier–Stokes equations") are a system of [partial differential equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partial_differential_equation "Partial differential equation") that has the [physical interpretation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fluid_dynamics "Fluid dynamics") of describing the velocity and pressure of certain [fluids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fluid "Fluid") in space, potentially in the presence of an external force (e.g. gravity). The [Euler equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euler_equations_(fluid_dynamics) "Euler equations (fluid dynamics)") are a special case of [Navier–Stokes equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Navier%E2%80%93Stokes_equations "Navier–Stokes equations") where the [viscosity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viscosity "Viscosity") of the fluid is zero, a property called [superfluidity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superfluidity "Superfluidity").<sup about="#mwt93" id="cite_ref-CFps_25-0" rel="dc:references" typeof="mw:Extension/ref" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Navier%E2%80%93Stokes_priority_controversy#cite_note-CFps-25" id="mwqQ"><span id="mwqg"><span id="mwqw">[</span>24<span id="mwrA">]</span></span></a></sup><sup about="#mwt89" typeof="mw:Transclusion" id="mwrQ" data-mw="{&quot;parts&quot;:[{&quot;template&quot;:{&quot;target&quot;:{&quot;wt&quot;:&quot;rp&quot;,&quot;href&quot;:&quot;./Template:Rp&quot;},&quot;params&quot;:{&quot;1&quot;:{&quot;wt&quot;:&quot;1&quot;}},&quot;i&quot;:0}}]}"><span title="Page / location: 1"><span typeof="mw:Entity">:</span><span typeof="mw:Entity"> </span>1<span typeof="mw:Entity"> </span></span></sup>  The [Navier–Stokes existence and smoothness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Navier%E2%80%93Stokes_existence_and_smoothness "Navier–Stokes existence and smoothness") problem, one of the [Millennium Prize Problems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Millennium_Prize_Problems "Millennium Prize Problems"), asks specifically whether "physically reasonable solutions exist" for these equations in the case where the fluid is [incompressible](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Incompressible_flow "Incompressible flow"), does not have superfluidity (i.e. the viscosity is positive), the space is [three-dimensional Each pair is in two different settings of the space being unbounded or periodic.<sup about="#mwt94" id="cite_ref-CFps_25-1" rel="dc:references" typeof="mw:Extension/ref" data-mw="{&quot;name&quot;:&quot;ref&quot;,&quot;attrs&quot;:{&quot;name&quot;:&quot;CFps&quot;}}"><a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Navier%E2%80%93Stokes_priority_controversy#cite_note-CFps-25" id="mwtg"><span id="mwtw"><span id="mwuA">[</span>24<span id="mwuQ">]</span></span></a></sup><sup about="#mwt90" typeof="mw:Transclusion" id="mwug" data-mw="{&quot;parts&quot;:[{&quot;template&quot;:{&quot;target&quot;:{&quot;wt&quot;:&quot;rp&quot;,&quot;href&quot;:&quot;./Template:Rp&quot;},&quot;params&quot;:{&quot;1&quot;:{&quot;wt&quot;:&quot;2&quot;}},&quot;i&quot;:0}}]}"><span title="Page / location: 2"><span typeof="mw:Entity">:</span><span typeof="mw:Entity"> </span>2<span typeof="mw:Entity"> </span></span></sup>

rumours about a mathematician's own work might prompt AI companies to invest huge efforts into pushing it instead of allowing the mathematician to develop their research to "full potential", whether the companies know about the details of the human mathematician's work or not.<sup about="#mwt281" id="cite_ref-59" rel="dc:references" typeof="mw:Extension/ref" expressed dissatisfaction with the current conduct of AI companies "using press releases or social media posts to communicate mathematical results" instead of uploading preprints or arranging talks at people that their passion for mathematics has no future", hence "extremely damaging to mathematics".<sup about="#mwt286" id="cite_ref-CZACscience_20-3" rel="dc:references" typeof="mw:Extension/ref"
