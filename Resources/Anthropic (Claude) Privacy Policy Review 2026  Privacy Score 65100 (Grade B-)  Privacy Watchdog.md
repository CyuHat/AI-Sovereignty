## What Should I Do About Claude and My Privacy?

Pick the row that matches you. These are the exact steps I would take, in order. Menu paths reflect Claude.ai and the Anthropic API as of mid-2026; if a label moved, look for the nearest equivalent under Settings.

Everyday user

### Lock down a personal account

1.  Open [Settings › Privacy](https://claude.ai/settings/data-privacy-controls) (profile menu, bottom left, then Privacy or Data controls).
2.  Turn the model-training setting **off**. The current Privacy Policy frames consumer training as opt-out, so it applies until you turn it off. Even after you opt out, the policy states safety-flagged or reported content is still used.
3.  Decline or shorten any extended data-retention option if you were offered one; choose the shorter default.
4.  Delete sensitive past chats individually, and use `Delete all conversations` in Settings to clear history.
5.  Never paste passwords, full account numbers, medical records, or another person's private data into any chat.

Business / professional user

### Keep work data out of the consumer pool

1.  Use the **Anthropic API or Claude for Work / Enterprise** for anything touching client, customer, or employee data. API and commercial inputs are not used to train models by default.
2.  Get a signed `DPA` (data processing addendum) and, if you handle health data, a `BAA` before sending regulated data.
3.  Set `Zero Data Retention` at the API/organization level where eligible, so prompts are not stored after the response.
4.  Write an internal AI-use policy: what may and may not be pasted into Claude.ai personal accounts.
5.  If you hold federal or defense contracts or subcontracts, read the Government & Defense Contractor Risk section below before any use.

Already affected

### Banned, exposed, or data already in

1.  Export everything now: Settings › Privacy › `Export data`, so you have a record before any further deletion.
2.  Submit a formal deletion / access request in writing and keep the timestamp and any ticket number.
3.  If you live in California, the EU, or another rights jurisdiction, cite your access and deletion rights in the request and note the statutory response window.
4.  Understand the hard limit: data already absorbed into a trained model generally cannot be pulled back out. Deletion stops future use, not past training.
5.  If an account was suspended or content was exposed and money or a contract is at stake, a written demand is the next escalation. See my [platform / data-deletion templates](https://terms.law/Demand-Letters/Platforms/).

**✅ Best move for most people** Toggle training off, set short retention, and delete sensitive chats. Five minutes, and it stops the largest privacy leak.

**⚠ The toggle does not cover everything** Safety-flagged conversations are retained longer regardless of your training opt-out. Treat any chat as potentially reviewable.

Enterprise buyer guide: privacy terms to demand in your MSA / DPA

If you are negotiating an AI vendor agreement, do not accept the click-through terms as-is. These are the provisions I press for in a master services agreement or data processing addendum before a business relies on any AI model:

-   **No-training warranty.** Written commitment that your inputs and outputs are not used to train or fine-tune models, with no carve-out for "service improvement."
-   **Retention and deletion SLA.** A defined retention period, a Zero Data Retention option where available, and a deletion-on-request commitment with a stated turnaround.
-   **Sub-processor transparency.** A current sub-processor list, advance notice of changes, and a right to object.
-   **Security and audit rights.** Current SOC 2 / ISO evidence, breach-notification timelines, and a contractual audit or audit-report right.
-   **Regulated-data coverage.** A signed BAA for PHI and explicit handling terms for cardholder or financial data, matched to your compliance obligations.
-   **Output IP and indemnity.** Clear ownership of outputs and a reasonable IP-infringement indemnity for model outputs.
-   **Jurisdiction and transfer terms.** Where data is processed, standard contractual clauses for cross-border transfer, and the governing law.

## ⚠ Government & Defense Contractor Risk

In early 2026 the Department of Defense designated Anthropic a “supply chain risk” under 10 U.S.C. § 3252 and the administration directed federal agencies to pause use of Claude, after Anthropic declined updated Pentagon contract terms over its limits on mass-surveillance and autonomous-weapons use ([Axios](https://www.axios.com/2026/03/09/anthropic-sues-pentagon-supply-chain-risk-label), [NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/tech/tech-news/anthropic-says-pentagon-declared-national-security-risk-rcna262013)). Anthropic sued, and on March 26, 2026 a federal court (N.D. Cal.) preliminarily enjoined the designation; the litigation is ongoing. Government procurement and national-security supply-chain rules can restrict, or even bar, federal agencies and defense contractors from using a given vendor, and those restrictions can arrive and change quickly. If you do business with the government, the relevant question is not the consumer privacy policy but your procurement and supply-chain compliance posture, and you should confirm Claude’s current federal availability against official guidance. Here is how I would frame the risk for an enterprise customer.

### Federal Agency Use

Federal agencies operate under procurement and security rules that can limit or prohibit a specific AI vendor. If your agency or program has integrated any AI model into a sensitive workflow, confirm the model's current approval status through your own contracting and security channels rather than assuming continuity.

### Defense Contractor Restrictions

A supply-chain or national-security designation can reach beyond direct government work and affect a contractor's internal use of the same vendor. If you hold defense contracts or subcontracts, do not assume a tool that is fine for commercial use is automatically cleared for your environment. Check your contract clauses and any active restrictions before deploying.

### Enterprise Compliance Risk

A company that holds federal or defense contracts or subcontracts can face compliance exposure from its AI vendor choices. In my view, enterprise legal teams with any defense-adjacent business should review their AI vendor relationships, because a supply-chain designation can create a chilling effect that reaches well beyond direct government contractors.

### What a Standoff Can Signal About Privacy

When an AI vendor is willing to walk away from a large customer rather than relax a stated policy commitment, I read that, in my framework's view, as evidence the company will enforce its principles under pressure. That is a positive signal for privacy-focused users. It is a signal, not a guarantee: the underlying commercial incentive to retain and learn from data does not disappear, so I still weight the operative terms over any public posture.

### Current Status

Government-vendor disputes of this kind can shape whether AI companies are able to maintain independent safety and privacy policies when those policies conflict with government demands. I track developments and update my analysis as they are confirmed.
